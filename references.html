Room-only scope and calculations
What the advertised room prices measure

The question is what one student is advertised a bedroom for. The article uses room, per-bedroom and single-person suite offers from NC State’s Off-Campus Housing portal. Whole-unit prices are excluded because the listing does not establish an individual student’s share. For the Main Campus comparison, manual review also excludes one listing that explicitly offered a bed in a shared bedroom and one listing whose bedroom occupancy could not be determined. The analysis does not divide apartment prices by an assumed number of roommates.

One observation is one portal listing ID. A community advertisement and a separate room advertisement can refer to the same building. The unit being counted is an advertisement, not a distinct building, tenant, available bed or signed lease. Advertisers supply the prices; the university does not guarantee their accuracy.

  1. Keep the snapshot fixed. Use the saved September 9 prices. The September 13 review clarifies classifications and exclusions without substituting newer live prices. Twenty saved titles say “Room in…” despite a whole-unit flag; three more descriptions establish room/suite offers. All 23 corrections are logged, including records that remain excluded for other reasons.
  2. Require a consistent price. Both endpoints must be numeric, positive and ordered low ≤ high. Exclude missing prices, unresolved mixed pricing bases, and disagreements between the saved listing range and floor-plan envelope. The floor plans check the listing price; they do not become extra observations.
  3. Choose the campus and band. Use the unrounded straight-line distance from the fixed campus point. A campus total includes its eligible room listings within five miles. The combined sample counts each eligible ID once across the three areas.
  4. Average comparable fields. Mean low = sum of low prices ÷ count. Mean high = sum of high prices ÷ the same count. Mean advertised room rent = sum of individual (low + high) ÷ 2 midpoints ÷ count. A fixed price has identical low and high endpoints.
  5. Round only the display. The article usually shows whole dollars; this audit shows cents. The low–high mean interval is neither the cheapest-to-most-expensive listing range nor a confidence interval. The median of listing midpoints is retained here and in Excel as a check.

Room counts by campus

Each campus’s three bands are separate. Campus totals overlap and cannot be added together. The article’s rent and earnings comparison starts with Main Campus; choosing another campus uses that campus’s room mean.

How the bands combine

Every listing contributes equally. Add all midpoints before dividing by the total count, or weight each unrounded band mean by its listing count. Giving the three bands equal weight would give listings in smaller groups more influence. The 0–1 mile row already shows the closest offers; no extra proximity weight is applied.

Geography
Straight-line distances, band edges and campus overlap

Reference coordinates (latitude, longitude): Main 35.77951, −78.68168; Centennial 35.76870, −78.67750; Centennial Biomedical 35.79847, −78.70408. These are fixed map reference points, not the nearest campus entrance or perimeter. The portal’s own travel-distance field is not used.

  1. Convert latitudes and longitudes from degrees to radians: radians = degrees × π ÷ 180.
  2. Calculate a = sin²(Δlatitude/2) + cos(latitude₁) × cos(latitude₂) × sin²(Δlongitude/2).
  3. Calculate miles = 2 × 3958.7613 × asin(√a). The equivalent atan2 form is used in the website build.
  4. Assign an unrounded distance: d ≤ 1; 1 < d ≤ 3; 3 < d ≤ 5. Never round a distance before deciding its band.

Example: Brook Hill is about 3.000166 miles from the Centennial point, just 0.87 feet outside the three-mile threshold. It belongs to the >3–5 band under these coordinates; geocoding precision can change such edge cases. The workbook shows feet to the nearest 1-, 3- or 5-mile edge and flags listings within 100 feet for inspection. This flag does not imply a measured geocoding error. Straight-line proximity does not establish walking, cycling, bus or driving time.

Interpretation
What the sample can support, and sensitivity checks

Suitable use: a descriptive report about advertised offers on this portal at the collection date, with the stated exclusions and earnings/aid assumptions. It is not a random sample of Raleigh rentals or NC State students. The 537 floor-plan rows are nested within the 168 listing records and are not 537 independent rental locations.

There is no universal minimum number of data points that makes a convenience sample representative. Some campus bands have few usable observations; use the displayed counts of prices used. A community and a single-room advertisement each receive one vote. Multiple advertisements in a building may be related, and unique listing IDs do not prove unique physical inventory. The means are not weighted by vacant units, bedrooms, residents or enrollment. No sampling margin of error or population-wide affordability rate is estimated.

Availability: the primary sample includes all advertised offers, including future move-ins, short leases, sublets and waitlist mentions. “Active” in the source is not proof of a vacant unit today. An all-2027 flag requires every saved floor-plan row to have a date in 2027 or later; missing dates and “Available Soon” are not assumed to mean available now. Lease and availability fields remain visible in the workbook. Removing all-2027 rows is a sensitivity check, not a verified available-now sample.

Costs: source price fields do not consistently establish the same fee coverage. Some floor plans contain higher “all-in” amounts. Mandatory, optional, usage-based and one-time fees are not interchangeable; do not add an arbitrary fee adjustment to every listing or double-count included utilities. Rents, concessions, furniture, parking and utilities need lease-level verification before making a personal housing decision. A deposit is an up-front cash requirement, not automatically a nonrefundable monthly expense.

Centennial Ridge’s portal explicitly labels $599–$2,157 per bedroom. It is retained as reported and separately flagged; the upper price is not divided by three on an unsupported assumption. Its omission is shown in the sensitivity table. Excluding disputed records can itself change the sample composition, so the reviewed subset is not guaranteed unbiased.

Claims needing more evidence: trends require comparable repeated snapshots; student burden or food insecurity requires student/household income and cost data; a campus-distance price effect needs controls for dwelling size, lease type, included services and amenities. This cross-section cannot establish those causal or population claims.

Earnings and aid comparison
What the aid checkboxes mean

The article starts with wages alone. The two optional checkboxes add NC State’s reported need-based grant average and need-based loan average. They are historical award benchmarks, not a standard FAFSA award or an estimate of a student’s refund.

Assumption: tuition and school fees are covered separately. Checking a box treats the full selected award as available for living expenses. Actual refunds may be smaller. The grant and loan averages describe different recipient groups; selecting both creates a hypothetical combination, not a typical aid package. Loans must be repaid. Food, books, transport and other expenses also compete for these funds.

Source figures

NC State Common Data Set 2025–26, Section H, reports 2024–25 final aid data. H2 K (PDF page 33) gives a $14,743 average need-based scholarship/grant award for full-time undergraduate recipients. H2 M (PDF page 34) gives a $4,106 average need-based loan for its full-time undergraduate recipients, excluding PLUS, unsubsidized and private alternative loans. Neither figure is an average across every NC State student or a 2026–27 award estimate.

NC State’s payment rules apply disbursed aid to billed charges and refund any excess. Dividing an academic-year award by 12 is a common-period comparison for this article, not a monthly disbursement schedule or evidence that the student receives summer aid.

Step-by-step calculation

  1. Gross monthly wages: hourly wage × weekly hours × 52 ÷ 12. At 20 hours/week, $15/hour gives $1,300.00/month and $7.25/hour gives $628.33/month. This assumes steady work throughout the year, before taxes.
  2. Grant equivalent, if checked: $14,743 ÷ 12 = $1,228.58/month. Otherwise add $0.
  3. Borrowed funds, if checked: $4,106 ÷ 12 = $342.17/month. Otherwise add $0.
  4. Monthly resources: gross wages + selected grant equivalent + selected borrowed funds. At $15/hour and 20 hours/week with both boxes checked, the total is $2,870.75/month.
  5. Rent share: the selected campus’s unrounded mean room midpoint ÷ monthly resources × 100. The examples below use Main Campus, matching the article’s default. A rounded percentage near 30% is labeled >30% or <30% when needed.

Work-study context

NC State Federal Work-Study lists a general $7.25–$15/hour range, with exceptions for higher pay, and generally advises no more than 20 hours/week. These are published guidelines, not a measurement of what every student worker earns. Actual work-study earnings are limited by the award and funded work period.

The wages above illustrate steady employment at those rates. They do not project year-round FWS funding. The separate 15-week column is simply wage × hours × 15: at 20 hours/week, $2,175 at $7.25 or $4,500 at $15, before any award cap. The broader workbook retains a separate calculation applying an actual award cap.

Hourly-pay control and calculation details

The left card stays at $7.25/hour; the right starts at $15/hour. Both use the same hours and aid selections. The right card accepts cents, while the minus and plus buttons change pay by $1. Reset $15 restores the default. A custom wage is a comparison, not a claim of FWS eligibility. The upper earnings table retains its fixed $7.25 and $15 rates.

The comparison uses 52 paid weeks and 12 months. Zero wages with no selected aid leave the percentage undefined. Invalid input retains the last valid calculation and shows a correction message. All calculations use unrounded inputs; rounding occurs only in the displayed result.

Affordability definition
HUD’s household benchmark versus this personal scenario

HUD CHAS background and definitions compares household housing costs, including utilities, with household income. More than 30% is cost burdened and more than 50% is severely cost burdened. At exactly 30%, the “more than 30%” threshold has not been crossed.

This article’s personal calculation can include a grant and loans, and its advertised rents do not consistently include all housing costs. It therefore reports a modeled share of monthly resources, not a formal HUD classification. Income includes all relevant household members for a household calculation; a whole dwelling’s cost should not be compared with one roommate’s income as if that were the household’s full income.

University benchmarks
Academic-year housing budgets and residence-hall equivalents

NC State’s 2026–27 undergraduate cost of attendance lists housing budgets of $8,738 on campus and $9,808 off campus for full-time fall and spring enrollment. These are budgeting allowances, not measured mean rents, twelve-month quotes or a promise that a student will find housing at that price.

University Housing’s projected 2026–27 rates: double room ($3,970 semester rent + $150 required ResNet) × 2 semesters = $8,240; $8,240 ÷ 9 months = $915.56/month per student. Single room ($4,600 + $150) × 2 = $9,500; $9,500 ÷ 9 = $1,055.56/month. Nine months is the display convention for an academic year, not nine equal monthly bills. Summer is excluded and winter-break arrangements may cost extra. On-campus furnishings, utilities and services differ from off-campus contracts.

Year-to-year room-rate change = (new semester rate − old semester rate) ÷ old semester rate × 100. Example for a double: ($3,970 − $3,800) ÷ $3,800 × 100 = 4.47%. The chart uses room charges alone in both years and excludes ResNet and Coastal Quarters communication fees; the monthly room examples include ResNet. National price context
August 2026 CPI benchmarks, with a fixed release link

The BLS August 2026 CPI release, published September 11, 2026 reports August-to-August increases of 3.1% for lodging while at school, 2.7% for rent of primary residence and 3.4% for all items. The reference uses this fixed August comparison; a rolling “latest release” link would eventually point to a different month.

BLS category definitions: lodging while at school covers college/university owned, leased or controlled housing. Merely advertising a private rental on the NC State referral portal does not put it in that CPI category. Rent of primary residence is the closer national comparison for private off-campus rents. These national twelve-month changes provide context; they do not estimate NC State’s local rent inflation or explain an individual room-rate increase.

Key fact for reporting

Actual $15/hour NC State position
NC State's Office of Undergraduate Research says students selected for its Federal Work-Study Research Assistant Program earn $15 per hour, up to the maximum allowed by the student's individual work-study award.
Article use: This is direct evidence that $15 per hour is an actual wage being paid in a current NC State Federal Work-Study program. It does not explain why NC State selected $15 as the normal upper end of its FWS wage guidelines, and it should not be described as a university-wide student minimum.

Key findings for reporting

Student-pay recommendation
The March 2024 report recommended a campus-wide salary study of student workers, including work-study positions, along with regular cost-of-living adjustments.
FWS wage concern
The report specifically discussed NC State's $7.25–$15 Federal Work-Study range and argued that, because universities have flexibility in setting work-study pay, the minimum should be raised significantly.
Article use: NC State-specific evidence that the university's low-end student wage was being questioned internally by 2024. This source supports the reporting question of why the FWS range still begins at $7.25; it does not explain why $15 was selected as the normal upper limit.

Step-by-step monthly equivalent math

Method note: NC State lists these as semester charges, not monthly rent. For comparison, the projected room rate is combined with the required $150 ResNet fee, doubled for fall + spring, and divided by a 9-month academic year. Coastal Quarters also adds its required $150 communication fee each semester. All figures are per student.
Residence hall double*
Low — Double: ($3,970 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $915.56/month ≈ $916
Calculated midpoint: (($3,970 + $4,350) ÷ 2 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $957.78/month ≈ $958
High — E.S. King 1-bedroom undergraduate double: ($4,350 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,000/month
Residence hall single
Low — Coastal Quarters single: ($4,350 + $150 ResNet + $150 communication) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,033.33/month ≈ $1,033
Calculated midpoint: ($1,033.33 + $1,055.56) ÷ 2 = $1,044.45/month ≈ $1,044
High — Single: ($4,600 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,055.56/month ≈ $1,056
* Double means a shared room occupied by two students. Residence-hall rates are charged per student, not per room.
