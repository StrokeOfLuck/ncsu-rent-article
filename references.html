Source type

Reference citation

North Carolina State University, Enrollment Management and Services. “Federal Work-Study Program.” Accessed Sept. 9, 2026.

Key facts for reporting

Allowable wage range
$7.25–$15.00 per hour. Employers may request exceptions for higher wages on a case-by-case basis.
Hours
NC State says students should generally work no more than 20 hours per week in Federal Work-Study positions.
Community service
Off-campus community-service Federal Work-Study positions are listed at $14.00 per hour.
How students are paid
Work-study earnings are paid directly to the student every two weeks, rather than automatically applied to the student account.
Funding split
The program states that Federal Work-Study covers 75% of earnings, while the employing unit covers the remaining 25%.
Wage-setting guidance
NC State says hourly wages should be based on job responsibilities and the student’s level of experience.
Article use: Primary source for the $7.25–$15 work-study wage comparison, the 20-hour work-study reference point, and the gross-earnings calculations used in the affordability graphic.

Reference citation

U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, HUD USER. “CHAS: Background.” Definitions of housing cost burden. Accessed Sept. 9, 2026.

Key facts for reporting

Cost burden
HUD defines a household as cost burdened when monthly housing costs, including utilities, exceed 30% of monthly income.
Severe cost burden
HUD defines severe cost burden as monthly housing costs, including utilities, exceeding 50% of monthly income.
Article use: Federal benchmark for interpreting the rent-burden percentages in the interactive. The 30% threshold is a housing-cost-burden benchmark, not a rule that every individual student must or should spend exactly 30% of income on rent.
