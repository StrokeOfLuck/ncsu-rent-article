Reference citation

What was collected

Portal search metadata
The unrestricted search reported 527 total results, 168 exact listings, 168 map pins and 5 result pages. These counts describe different parts of the portal and are not treated as interchangeable.
Unique properties
The scraper collected all 168 unique exact property records returned by the unrestricted search.
Property details
A detail request was successfully collected for all 168 properties, producing 537 floor-plan rows.
Map reporting unit
The map uses the unique property/location as its reporting unit rather than counting every floor plan as a separate rental location.
Price treatment
Per-bedroom and whole-unit prices are kept separate. Listings without a positive numeric advertised price are excluded from the corresponding price average rather than treated as zero.
Distance treatment
Distances used in the interactive are recalculated from each listing's latitude and longitude as straight-line geographic distance. The portal's supplied campus-distance field is not used for the radius calculations.
Combined affordability summary
The overall affordability visualizer is not tied to the currently selected map campus. It pools every unique listing appearing in any Main Campus, Centennial Campus or Centennial Biomedical Campus (CVM reference view) 1-, 3- or 5-mile view, then counts each NC State Off-Campus Housing listing once before averaging rents.
Worked calculation for the affordability visualizer: The nine displayed campus-radius views contain 156 unique listings after deduplication by NC State Off-Campus Housing listing ID: 67 per-bedroom listings and 89 whole-unit listings. All 67 per-bedroom listings include numeric rent values; 88 of the 89 whole-unit listings include numeric low/high rent values and are used in the whole-unit average. The remaining whole-unit listing has no numeric advertised rent in the collected data. A property that appears in multiple overlapping views is counted once. Calculation method: add the numeric low rents for the unique listings and divide by the number of listings with a numeric low rent; repeat separately for the high rents. The three 1-, 3- and 5-mile summary rows are not averaged together because those radius groups overlap and would count many of the same listings more than once.

Per bedroom: low-end advertised rents total $55,492.00. $55,492.00 ÷ 67 = $828.24. High-end advertised rents total $63,270.00. $63,270.00 ÷ 67 = $944.33. Midpoint = ($828.24 + $944.33) ÷ 2 = $886.28. Article display after rounding to whole dollars: $828–$944; midpoint $886.

Whole unit: low-end advertised rents total $147,564.00. $147,564.00 ÷ 88 = $1,676.86. High-end advertised rents total $162,590.00. $162,590.00 ÷ 88 = $1,847.61. Midpoint = ($1,676.86 + $1,847.61) ÷ 2 = $1,762.24. Article display after rounding to whole dollars: $1,677–$1,848; midpoint $1,762.

Why this is the combined set: the 1- and 3-mile views are subsets of each campus's 5-mile view. Combining all nine displayed views and deduplicating produces the same 156 unique listings as taking the union of the three 5-mile views. The calculation above is written as all nine views because that matches what the article displays.
Non-overlapping distance-band method: The article's distance comparison uses 0–1 mile, more than 1–3 miles, and more than 3–5 miles from the selected campus reference point. A listing can appear in only one band within a campus comparison. Distances are straight-line Haversine calculations from the campus reference point. Each mean is calculated directly from the listings in that confined band: sum the numeric low rents and divide by the number of numeric low rents, then repeat for the high rents. The band means are not produced by subtracting or averaging the cumulative-radius means.
Worked calculation for the selected-campus off-campus summary card: The card beneath the distance-band table gives the three non-overlapping bands equal weight. For each pricing type: (0–1 mile mean + >1–3 mile mean + >3–5 mile mean) ÷ 3. The same calculation is done separately for the low and high rent means; the midpoint is halfway between those results. Bands are not weighted by their number of listings.

What the “one-third rule” actually means

What the 30% rule measures
HUD uses the share of income spent on housing as a simple affordability measure. Spending 30% or less is treated as no cost burden; spending more than 30% is cost burdened, and spending more than 50% is severely cost burdened.
What counts as housing cost
HUD's cost-burden definition uses monthly housing costs including utilities.
Why the threshold matters
HUD explains that a household paying more than 30% of income toward housing may have difficulty affording necessities such as food, clothing, transportation and medical care. The 30% line is therefore a cost-burden warning threshold, not a federal definition of comfort, financial security or “thriving,” and it does not mean households should aim to spend exactly 30%.
College-student caveat
The benchmark is useful context for student housing, but students can have grants, loans, savings, family support, roommates and other resources that do not map neatly onto ordinary household income. The article should therefore use the 30% line as an affordability comparison rather than claim that every student above it is formally HUD cost burdened.
Article / interactive use: Red in the affordability visualizer marks a modeled rent share above 30%. This is a comparison with HUD's housing-affordability benchmark. It is not a formal HUD classification when the interactive's denominator includes grants or borrowed money, and the rental listings may not include utilities.

Plain-language HUD definition

“A household is said to be cost-burdened when it pays more than 30% of its income toward housing expenses and may have difficulty affording necessities such as food, clothing, transportation, and medical care.”
HUD FY24 ICDBG FAQs, question 29, page 8. Screenshot saved for the reporting record.
Reporting language: Prefer “above HUD's 30% housing-affordability benchmark” or “above the 30% cost-burden threshold” over saying a student “fails the one-third rule.” The commonly repeated “one-third rule” is only shorthand; the federal benchmark used here is 30%.

Step-by-step monthly equivalent math

Method note: NC State lists these as semester charges, not monthly rent. For comparison, the projected room rate is combined with the required $150 ResNet fee, doubled for fall + spring, and divided by a 9-month academic year. Coastal Quarters also adds its required $150 communication fee each semester. All figures are per student.
Residence hall double*
Low — Double: ($3,970 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $915.56/month ≈ $916
Calculated midpoint: (($3,970 + $4,350) ÷ 2 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $957.78/month ≈ $958
High — E.S. King 1-bedroom undergraduate double: ($4,350 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,000/month
Residence hall single
Low — Coastal Quarters single: ($4,350 + $150 ResNet + $150 communication) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,033.33/month ≈ $1,033
Calculated midpoint: ($1,033.33 + $1,055.56) ÷ 2 = $1,044.45/month ≈ $1,044
High — Single: ($4,600 + $150) × 2 ÷ 9 = $1,055.56/month ≈ $1,056
* Double means a shared room occupied by two students. Residence-hall rates are charged per student, not per room.
