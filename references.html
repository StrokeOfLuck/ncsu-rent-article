Reference citation

North Carolina State University. “Off-Campus Housing.” Rental listings advertised through NC State's Off-Campus Housing website, collected Sept. 9, 2026.

What was collected

Portal search metadata
The unrestricted search reported 527 total results, 168 exact listings, 168 map pins and 5 result pages. These counts describe different parts of the portal and are not treated as interchangeable.
Unique properties
The scraper collected all 168 unique exact property records returned by the unrestricted search.
Property details
A detail request was successfully collected for all 168 properties, producing 537 floor-plan rows.
Map reporting unit
The map uses the unique property/location as its reporting unit rather than counting every floor plan as a separate rental location.
Price treatment
Per-bedroom and whole-unit prices are kept separate. Listings without a positive numeric advertised price are excluded from the corresponding price average rather than treated as zero.
Distance treatment
Distances used in the interactive are recalculated from each listing's latitude and longitude as straight-line geographic distance. The portal's supplied campus-distance field is not used for the radius calculations.
Combined affordability summary
The overall affordability visualizer is not tied to the currently selected map campus. It averages the displayed per-bedroom and whole-unit rent summaries across Main Campus, Centennial Campus and Vet School for the 1-, 3- and 5-mile views, giving nine campus-radius summary ranges in each pricing category.
Article use: This is the source and collection record for the rental data shown on the map. Preferred wording is “Listings advertised through NC State's Off-Campus Housing website, collected on September 9, 2026.” The dataset should not be described as every rental available around NC State or Raleigh.
Reproducibility and privacy: The scraper runs in the browser while the NC State housing page is open and does not save copied cookies or login credentials. The public repository includes cleaned analysis files and the scraper, but not the lossless raw response archive. Listings marked by the portal to hide an address have exact street-address text removed from the public checkpoint.

North Carolina State University, Enrollment Management and Services. “Federal Work-Study Program.” Accessed Sept. 9, 2026.

Key facts for reporting

Allowable wage range
$7.25–$15.00 per hour. Employers may request exceptions for higher wages on a case-by-case basis.
Hours
NC State says students should generally work no more than 20 hours per week in Federal Work-Study positions.
Community service
Off-campus community-service Federal Work-Study positions are listed at $14.00 per hour.
How students are paid
Work-study earnings are paid directly to the student every two weeks, rather than automatically applied to the student account.
Funding split
The program states that Federal Work-Study covers 75% of earnings, while the employing unit covers the remaining 25%.
Wage-setting guidance
NC State says hourly wages should be based on job responsibilities and the student’s level of experience.
Article use: Primary source for the $7.25–$15 work-study wage comparison, the 20-hour work-study reference point, and the gross-earnings calculations used in the affordability graphic.

Reference citation

U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, HUD USER. “CHAS: Background.” Definitions of housing cost burden. Accessed Sept. 9, 2026.

Key facts for reporting

Cost burden
HUD defines a household as cost burdened when monthly housing costs, including utilities, exceed 30% of monthly income.
Severe cost burden
HUD defines severe cost burden as monthly housing costs, including utilities, exceeding 50% of monthly income.
Article use: Federal benchmark for interpreting the rent-burden percentages in the interactive. The 30% threshold is a housing-cost-burden benchmark, not a rule that every individual student must or should spend exactly 30% of income on rent.

HUD 30% Housing Affordability Benchmark
Federal source HUD 30% benchmark Affordability
Organization
U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, HUD USER
Primary page
HUD USER Glossary — Affordable Housing
Definition page
HUD CHAS — Cost burden definition
Accessed
Sept. 9, 2026
Source type
Federal housing-affordability definition and methodological guidance

What the “one-third rule” actually means

Official threshold
HUD's standard is 30% of income, not exactly one third (33.3%). HUD defines housing costs above 30% of monthly income as a housing cost burden.
What counts as housing cost
HUD's cost-burden definition uses monthly housing costs including utilities.
Rule of thumb, not a mandate
HUD's glossary describes the 30% affordability standard as an approximate guideline or general rule of thumb. It is not a legal requirement that every household or student spend 30% or less.
College-student caveat
The benchmark is useful context for student housing, but students can have grants, loans, savings, family support, roommates and other resources that do not map neatly onto ordinary household income. The article should therefore use the 30% line as an affordability comparison rather than claim that every student above it is formally HUD cost burdened.
Article / interactive use: Red in the affordability visualizer marks a modeled rent share above 30%. This is a comparison with HUD's housing-affordability benchmark. It is not a formal HUD classification when the interactive's denominator includes grants or borrowed money, and the rental listings may not include utilities.
Reporting language: Prefer “above HUD's 30% housing-affordability benchmark” or “above the 30% cost-burden threshold” over saying a student “fails the one-third rule.” The commonly repeated “one-third rule” is only shorthand; the federal benchmark used here is 30%.
