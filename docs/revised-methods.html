Rental data & calculations
What this snapshot measures, and how every rent is calculated
Revised September 13, 2026

Reporting unit: one portal listing ID. These are advertised monthly prices collected September 9, 2026 from NC State’s Off-Campus Housing portal. Listings are supplied by landlords and other advertisers; the university does not guarantee their accuracy. One building may have a community advertisement and separate room or sublet advertisements. We do not call the records distinct buildings, tenants, available beds or signed leases.

The saved CSVs contain 168 unique listing IDs and 537 floor-plan rows. The earlier collection log recorded 168 exact results/map pins, five pages and a separate 527-result portal total. The saved data verify 168 records, not completeness of all 527 results or the Raleigh market. The original raw backend response archive is not in this repository; the derived CSVs and their hashes are preserved. Collection timestamp reported in the original source log: 2026-09-09T02:24:45.953Z. A listing’s “last updated” field is not its collection timestamp.

  1. Keep the snapshot fixed. Preserve the original property and floor-plan CSVs. A September 13 source check may clarify whether an offer is a room; it does not replace a September 9 price with a newer live price.
  2. Separate pricing bases. A room or per-bedroom offer is a personal housing offer, including suites with shared facilities. A whole-unit offer prices a complete dwelling. Twenty saved titles explicitly say “Room in…” despite a whole-unit flag. Three additional descriptions establish room/suite offers. All 23 overrides and their evidence are recorded. Unresolved mixed or questionable price bases remain in the review group.
  3. Use a positive, complete price pair. Both low and high must be numeric, greater than zero and ordered low ≤ high. “Call for pricing,” blank and zero placeholders are missing values. Search-summary and saved floor-plan envelopes that disagree are excluded until reconciled. The floor-plan envelope is a consistency check, not a replacement price. A difference may reflect fees, time or plan scope rather than a scraper error.
  4. Choose the geography. Calculate straight-line distance from each fixed campus reference point. For the combined sample, keep IDs within five miles of at least one point and deduplicate once by ID. For a campus card, use all eligible listings within five miles of that campus. Each listing contributes once per relevant category; bands receive weight in proportion to their eligible counts.
  5. Calculate the endpoint means. Low mean = sum of included low prices ÷ included price pairs. High mean = sum of included high prices ÷ the same count. Mean midpoint = (low mean + high mean) ÷ 2, which equals the mean of individual listing midpoints. Median midpoint = the middle individual midpoint, or the average of the two middle values for an even count.
  6. Round only for display. Calculate from unrounded values. References show cents and the article usually shows whole dollars. The low-to-high mean interval is neither the sample’s minimum-to-maximum range nor a confidence interval.

How the article's counts add up

The article's map, distance table and campus summary focus on usable room/per-bedroom listings. Add the three nonoverlapping room counts to get that campus's total. The affordability chart uses the mean listing midpoint; medians and whole-unit calculations remain here and in Excel for comparison. Whole-unit offers are valid audit data, but do not establish one student's rent share. The broader snapshot totals below include both price categories and excluded prices.

Do not add campus totals together: their five-mile areas overlap. The combined sample counts each listing ID once. Whole-unit and room/per-bedroom prices have separate means because they price different things.

Should closer rentals have more weight?

Closeness matters when choosing housing. The overall average answers a specific question: what are the average advertised prices among the included listings within five miles? Each included listing has one equal contribution. To focus on the closest offers, use the 0–1 mile row directly.

A proximity-weighted average would answer a different question. It would require a published rule, such as how much more a listing at one mile counts than one at three miles, and a reason for that choice. This snapshot does not measure students' location preferences or where they actually live, so it does not establish those weights. Giving every distance band the same weight also changes the result: a listing in a band with fewer prices would count more.

For the current average, each band contributes in proportion to its usable price count. Pool all listing prices before dividing, or multiply each band's unrounded average by its usable count, add those products, and divide by the total usable count. Keep room and whole-unit calculations separate.

Every campus and distance band, with sums and denominators

Each campus has three disjoint bands: 0–1, more than 1–3, and more than 3–5 miles. “Total listings” includes excluded prices. “Prices used” is the count used to divide both sums. Room prices used + whole-unit prices used + excluded listings = total listings. The total and excluded counts repeat for the two category rows; do not add those repeated counts together.

Geography
Straight-line distances, band edges and campus overlap

Reference coordinates (latitude, longitude): Main 35.77951, −78.68168; Centennial 35.76870, −78.67750; Centennial Biomedical 35.79847, −78.70408. These are fixed map reference points, not the nearest campus entrance or perimeter. The portal’s own travel-distance field is not used.

  1. Convert latitudes and longitudes from degrees to radians: radians = degrees × π ÷ 180.
  2. Calculate a = sin²(Δlatitude/2) + cos(latitude₁) × cos(latitude₂) × sin²(Δlongitude/2).
  3. Calculate miles = 2 × 3958.7613 × asin(√a). The equivalent atan2 form is used in the website build.
  4. Assign an unrounded distance: d ≤ 1; 1 < d ≤ 3; 3 < d ≤ 5. Never round a distance before deciding its band.

Example: Brook Hill is about 3.000166 miles from the Centennial point, just 0.87 feet outside the three-mile threshold. It belongs to the >3–5 band under these coordinates; geocoding precision can change such edge cases. The workbook shows feet to the nearest 1-, 3- or 5-mile edge and flags listings within 100 feet for inspection. This flag does not imply a measured geocoding error. Straight-line proximity does not establish walking, cycling, bus or driving time.

NC State Facilities GIS perimeter and building layers provide visual context, not the distance origin. Saved perimeters · Saved buildings. Earlier Kepler exports are retained as historical distance artifacts in the repository; the reviewed checker and workbook are the current price audit.

Interpretation
What the sample can support, and sensitivity checks

Suitable use: a descriptive report about advertised offers on this portal at the collection date, with the stated exclusions and budget assumptions. It is not a random sample of Raleigh rentals or NC State students. The 537 floor-plan rows are nested within the 168 listing records and are not 537 independent rental locations.

There is no universal minimum number of data points that makes a convenience sample representative. Some campus bands have few usable observations; use the displayed counts of prices used. A community and a single-room advertisement each receive one vote. Multiple advertisements in a building may be related, and unique listing IDs do not prove unique physical inventory. The means are not weighted by vacant units, bedrooms, residents or enrollment. No sampling margin of error or population-wide affordability rate is estimated.

Availability: the primary sample includes all advertised offers, including future move-ins, short leases, sublets and waitlist mentions. “Active” in the source is not proof of a vacant unit today. An all-2027 flag requires every saved floor-plan row to have a date in 2027 or later; missing dates and “Available Soon” are not assumed to mean available now. Lease and availability fields remain visible in the workbook. Removing all-2027 rows is a sensitivity check, not a verified available-now sample.

Costs: source price fields do not consistently establish the same fee coverage. Some floor plans contain higher “all-in” amounts. Mandatory, optional, usage-based and one-time fees are not interchangeable; do not add an arbitrary fee adjustment to every listing or double-count included utilities. Rents, concessions, furniture, parking and utilities need lease-level verification before making a personal housing decision. A deposit is an up-front cash requirement, not automatically a nonrefundable monthly expense.

Centennial Ridge’s portal explicitly labels $599–$2,157 per bedroom. It is retained as reported and separately flagged; the upper price is not divided by three on an unsupported assumption. Its omission is shown below the primary result. Excluding disputed records can itself change the sample composition, so the reviewed subset is not guaranteed unbiased.

Claims needing more evidence: trends require comparable repeated snapshots; student burden or food insecurity requires student/household income and cost data; a campus-distance price effect needs controls for dwelling size, lease type, included services and amenities. This cross-section cannot establish those causal or population claims.

Aid checkboxes & budget arithmetic
Compare with average need-based aid, without entering a personal budget

The article starts with work pay only. Readers can check Need-based grant average, Need-based loan average, or both. The presets use published recipient averages and spread the selected annual amount over 12 months. No personal information is needed.

Preset assumption: tuition and school fees are paid separately. Selecting a checkbox models the full average award as available for living expenses. It does not claim that an average student actually receives that amount as a refund. The two averages come from different recipient groups; selecting both is a hypothetical combination, not an observed typical package. Loans are borrowed funds that must be repaid. Food, books and transport also compete for these resources.

Step by step

  1. Calculate monthly gross work pay. Hourly wage × selected weekly hours × 52 paid weeks ÷ 12 months. At $15 × 20 × 52 ÷ 12 = $1,300.00/month. At $7.25 × 20 × 52 ÷ 12 = $628.33/month. This steady-work scenario is before taxes and does not guarantee year-round work-study employment.
  2. Add a grant only if checked. $14,743 ÷ 12 = $1,228.58/month equivalent. If unchecked, add $0.
  3. Add a loan only if checked. $4,106 ÷ 12 = $342.17/month borrowed. If unchecked, add $0.
  4. Add the selected resources. Monthly resources = gross work pay + selected grant equivalent + selected borrowed funds. At $15/hour and 20 hours/week with both boxes checked: $1,300 + ($14,743 + $4,106) ÷ 12 = $2,870.75/month.
  5. Compare room rent. Room mean ÷ monthly resources × 100. The median is retained here as a comparison. The following table uses the same unrounded values as the article. Labels in the article are rounded; a value just above or below 30% is labeled >30% or <30% so rounding does not conceal which side of the reference it falls on.

Optional: use a personal budget

Open Use your own budget (optional) and check Use custom amounts instead of the award averages. This pauses the average-award boxes. Enter only aid actually left for living expenses after tuition, fees, account charges and other commitments. Use the same budget period for all totals. Closing the optional panel does not switch modes; the visible status states when the custom budget is in use.

Custom work pay = wage × weekly hours × paid weeks ÷ budget months. Example: $15 × 20 × 30 paid weeks ÷ 9 months = $1,000/month. If $1,800 of grant and $900 of loan funds remain for that period, add $200 and $100 per month: total resources = $1,300/month. The calculator limits paid weeks to 52 × months ÷ 12 so the schedule fits the period. Additional housing costs default to zero; do not add charges already included in rent. With zero resources, the share is undefined and displays a dash.

Whole-unit sharing: use a specific lease and actual payers. Example: ($2,400 rent + $180 shared utilities) ÷ 3 equal payers = $860 per person. $860 ÷ $1,300 = 66.15%. Three payers is an explicit assumption, not inferred from a community’s range of apartment sizes. The workbook provides this separate example; the article’s personal chart uses room/per-bedroom offers.

Financial-aid source and period

NC State Common Data Set 2025–26, Section H, selects 2024–25 final data. H2 K reports the $14,743 average need-based scholarship/grant award for full-time undergraduate recipients; H2 M reports the $4,106 average need-based loan for its full-time undergraduate recipients. These are historical recipient means, not medians, amounts for every student, or 2026–27 estimates.

NC State’s financial-aid payment rules: aid first pays eligible student-account charges and any excess is refunded. That is why the easy checkbox illustration explicitly assumes tuition and fees are paid separately, and why the custom option uses only the remaining living-expense amount.

Work-study award cap

NC State Federal Work-Study gives a general $7.25–$15 hourly range, with exceptions, and generally limits work to 20 hours a week. Actual earnings depend on hours, funded weeks and the award cap. Fifteen weeks at 20 hours gives $2,175 at $7.25 or $4,500 at $15 before applying that cap. Maximum FWS pay for the period = the smaller of modeled earnings and the remaining award. The Budget sheet includes this separate cap calculation.

Affordability definition
HUD’s household benchmark versus this personal scenario

HUD CHAS background and definitions compares household housing costs, including utilities, with household income. More than 30% is cost burdened and more than 50% is severely cost burdened. At exactly 30%, the “more than 30%” threshold has not been crossed.

This article’s personal calculation can include a grant, outside support and loans, and its advertised rents do not consistently include all housing costs. It therefore reports a modeled share of monthly resources, not a formal HUD classification. Income includes all relevant household members for a household calculation; a whole dwelling’s cost should not be compared with one roommate’s income as if that were the household’s full income.

University benchmarks
Academic-year housing budgets and residence-hall equivalents

NC State’s 2026–27 undergraduate cost of attendance lists housing budgets of $8,738 on campus and $9,808 off campus for full-time fall and spring enrollment. These are budgeting allowances, not measured mean rents, twelve-month quotes or a promise that a student will find housing at that price.

University Housing’s projected 2026–27 rates: double room ($3,970 semester rent + $150 required ResNet) × 2 semesters = $8,240; $8,240 ÷ 9 months = $915.56/month per student. Single room ($4,600 + $150) × 2 = $9,500; $9,500 ÷ 9 = $1,055.56/month. Nine months is the display convention for an academic year, not nine equal monthly bills. Summer is excluded and winter-break arrangements may cost extra. On-campus furnishings, utilities and services differ from off-campus contracts.

Year-to-year room-rate change = (new semester rate − old semester rate) ÷ old semester rate × 100. Example for a double: ($3,970 − $3,800) ÷ $3,800 × 100 = 4.47%. The chart uses room charges alone in both years and excludes ResNet and Coastal Quarters communication fees; the monthly room examples include ResNet. National price context
July 2026 CPI benchmarks, with a fixed release link

The BLS July 2026 CPI release, published August 12, 2026 reports July-to-July increases of 3.0% for lodging while at school, 2.9% for rent of primary residence and 3.4% for all items. The article intentionally uses this fixed July comparison; a rolling “latest release” link would eventually point to a different month.

BLS category definitions: lodging while at school covers college/university owned, leased or controlled housing. Merely advertising a private rental on the NC State referral portal does not put it in that CPI category. Rent of primary residence is the closer national comparison for private off-campus rents. These national twelve-month changes provide context; they do not estimate NC State’s local rent inflation or explain an individual room-rate increase.
